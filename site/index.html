Infinite context, by design

Our models and agents do not run out of room. A single action is bounded by an Action Context of about a million tokens of output, and a turn of thinking is bounded by an Attention Span of about a million tokens of reasoning and memory. Everything around those two numbers rolls forward without an end.

There is no compaction, no compression, and no project too large to hand over. Nothing is rewritten to make room: the window rolls forward and the context keeps going, so work can run for months without a summarisation step in the middle of it.

Easter Company

Operated by husband and wife, Owen and Hannah Easter. We build products across engineering, entertainment, education and enterprise, with a focus on productivity, management, AI and gaming.

Easter Company was founded on February 1st, 2016. Our first product was VI Mark 1 (MK I), our first AI model, and it was significantly more advanced than anything else of its time, far closer to the modern language models that would follow years later.

MK II is where the name stuck. We officially adopted “Dexter” for the AI because Owen had heard one too many jokes from family and friends about him building himself an AI girlfriend. True story.

Dexter MK IV, through 2017 and 2018, was a fully capable smart home and smart device orchestrator, able to control any Linux based system by voice command. It was free and available online, and it never saw much success for the simple reason that hardly anybody ever saw it. The people who did were never less than impressed, and Easter Company kept funding its AI research the way we still do: through general software design and development contracts.

Between 2018 and 2020 we developed the Overlord Framework, a web and native app development framework. By that point we had re-engineered every wheel there was, and this framework put all of the pieces together in a C, Python and TypeScript environment we controlled entirely. It let our Dexter AI model excel much faster, and to a far higher standard, than competing models, because the goal it was given was far simpler. Dexter was learning to use Python functions and call them inside a framework we owned end to end, and it was building entire contracted codebases, worth thousands of dollars, in minutes and for less than a penny.

GitHub Copilot, a multi-million dollar project, showed us how a worse developer would solve the problem we were about to solve. Dexter still never quite got seen online, but its abilities were put to very good use inside our own software development.

Now we are going even harder, with three brand-new and fully capable agentic models, ten times cheaper than the competition and a hundred times smarter. We are here to solve AI for everyone, and the company mission is this: “We believe we’re really good at AI, and everything we’ve seen come from the big tech world is really disappointing to us, so we’re going to step-up and sort it out now.”
